## 1. Statutory & Maritime Law Foundations (Legal Admissibility)

Evidence generated by Ocean Shield is structured to meet evidentiary admissibility standards under the **Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (Section 65B: Electronic Records)** and the following international treaties:

1. **MARPOL 73/78 Annex I (Regulations for the Prevention of Pollution by Oil):**
   * *Regulation 15 & 34:* Discharges outside special areas are strictly prohibited unless vessel is proceeding en route, instantaneous discharge rate does not exceed 30 liters per nautical mile, and oil content of effluent without dilution does not exceed 15 ppm.
   * *Legal Nexus:* Our 5-factor Bayesian matrix establishes the kinematic "discharge window" (<3 knots, route deviation, darkness/nighttime pumping).

2. **United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS, 1982):**
   * *Article 194:* Measures to prevent, reduce and control pollution of the marine environment.
   * *Article 211:* Laws and regulations concerning maritime pollution from vessels.
   * *Article 217 & 220:* Enforcement by coastal States in the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) up to 200 nautical miles.

3. **Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 (Act No. 44 of 1958):**
   * *Part XIA (Sections 356A–356O):* Prevention and containment of pollution of the sea by oil; empowering the Director General of Shipping and the Indian Coast Guard to detain offending vessels.

4. **IMO Resolution A.917(22) (Guidelines for Shipborne AIS):**
   * Mandates continuous VHF broadcast of maritime mobile service identity (MMSI), coordinates, SOG, COG, heading, and navigational status.
   * *Spoofing & Disabling Defense:* Ocean Shield detects intentional AIS transponder blackout zones when transiting high-risk EEZ corridors.
